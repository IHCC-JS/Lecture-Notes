Who Was Laika?

Laika was a stray dog found on the streets of Moscow in 1957. She became the first living being to orbit Earth aboard the Soviet spacecraft Sputnik 2.

Training and Selection

Soviet scientists chose small, calm, and obedient stray dogs for early space missions. Laika was one of three dogs considered, along with Albina and Mushka.

  • She was trained in small spaces to adapt to the capsule.
  • She wore a custom-fitted harness and suit.
  • Food was provided in gelatin form.

Few records remain of her exact training regimen, but it is known that she remained gentle throughout.

The Sputnik 2 Mission

Launch

On November 3, 1957, the Soviet Union launched Sputnik 2 from the Baikonur Cosmodrome. The mission was designed to test life-support systems for potential human flights.

Conditions in Space

Sensors recorded Laika's heartbeat, breathing, and movement. The cabin contained oxygen and temperature controls, but they soon failed.

Outcome

Laika perished a few hours after launch due to overheating. The spacecraft continued to orbit Earth for 162 days before burning up upon reentry.

Legacy

Laika's mission demonstrated that living creatures could survive launch and microgravity. Though her death caused worldwide sadness, it advanced human spaceflight research.

In 2008, Russia built a small monument near Moscow in her honor, featuring Laika standing atop a rocket.

"Laika's courage opened the path to the stars."
